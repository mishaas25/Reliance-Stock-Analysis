
**Image 1 — Overall Stock Price Trend (Close Price Over Time)**
Reliance Industries showed a strong and consistent upward trend from ~₹500 in 2016 to over ₹2,300 by late 2021. The stock experienced a brief but sharp crash to ~₹880 in early 2020 (COVID-19 market panic), followed by one of its sharpest recoveries, nearly tripling in price within 18 months. The overall trajectory confirms Reliance as a dominant long-term compounder on the NSE.

**Image 2 — Distribution of Intraday Price Changes (Close - Open)**
Intraday price changes (Close minus Open) are tightly centered around ₹0, forming a near-perfect bell curve. The distribution has slightly heavier tails on the negative side, indicating that sharp intraday selloffs occur marginally more often than equivalent upward surges. Most daily intraday moves fall within a ±₹50 range, reflecting generally orderly price discovery.

**Image 3 — Trading Volume Over Time**
Volume was relatively moderate and spiky during 2016–2019, with occasional sharp bursts around major corporate events. The most dramatic volume surge occurred in early 2020 during the COVID crash, with daily volumes exceeding 65 million shares — the highest in the entire dataset. Post-2020, volumes remained structurally elevated compared to pre-2020 levels, reflecting increased retail and institutional participation following Reliance's landmark rights issue and Jio-related deals.

**Image 4 — Average Monthly Closing Price Distribution (Pie Chart)**
The monthly share of average closing prices is remarkably uniform across all 12 months, each contributing roughly 7.8%–9.2% of the total. June (9.2%) and July (8.9%) carry slightly higher weight, likely due to elevated prices in those months across the dataset years. August (7.8%) and November (7.9%) are the lightest months. The near-equal distribution suggests no single month dramatically dominates Reliance's price history.

**Image 5 — Year-over-Year Change in Average Close Price**
Every year from 2017 to 2021 showed positive year-over-year growth in average closing price, confirming an uninterrupted multi-year bull run. Growth was strongest in 2017 (+42%) and 2018 (+41%), reflecting early-stage re-rating of the stock. Growth moderated to +27% in 2019 and rebounded to +33% in 2020 despite the COVID crash — a testament to the strength of the post-crash recovery. By 2021, YoY growth slowed to +18% as the base effect from 2020's surge set in.

**Image 6 — 30-Day Rolling Volatility Over Time**
Volatility was relatively contained between 1%–2.5% throughout 2016–2019, with minor spikes around corporate events. The COVID-19 market crash in March 2020 triggered a massive volatility spike to ~6.6% — more than double any prior level — representing the most extreme risk episode in this dataset. Volatility quickly normalized post-mid-2020, declining back toward 1%–2% levels through 2021, as markets stabilized and Reliance's fundamentals reasserted confidence.

**Image 7 — Distribution of Daily Returns (%)**
Daily percentage returns are approximately normally distributed and centered just above 0%, consistent with the stock's overall positive long-term drift. The distribution has mild fat tails on both sides, with extreme returns occasionally exceeding ±10%. The slight right skew suggests positive surprise days (large gains) are marginally more frequent than equivalent large losses, a favorable characteristic for long-term holders.

**Image 8 — Intraday Change Distribution by Year (Violin Plot)**
The violin plots reveal a clear widening of intraday price swings over time. From 2016 to 2019, the distributions are narrow and tightly clustered around zero. In 2020, the violin expands dramatically — extending from +₹150 to -₹170 — reflecting the COVID crash's extreme intraday volatility. By 2021, the distribution remains wider than pre-2020 years but narrows compared to 2020, indicating a partial return to normalcy while still carrying elevated intraday risk at higher price levels.

**Image 9 — Daily Trading Range Distribution by Year (Box Plot)**
The daily High-Low range (a direct measure of intraday volatility) grew steadily from a median of ~₹8 in 2016 to ~₹20 in 2019. In 2020, the median range exploded to ~₹47 with extreme outliers exceeding ₹190, driven by the COVID panic. 2021 shows a median range of ~₹40, remaining significantly higher than pre-2020 years — reflecting the higher absolute price level and continued market sensitivity to news flow around Reliance's ongoing business transformation.

**Image 10 — Close Price vs. 50-Day & 200-Day Moving Averages**
The 50-day SMA closely tracks short-term price movements while the 200-day SMA confirms the long-term uptrend. A Golden Cross (50-day crossing above the 200-day) was visible around early 2017 and again in mid-2020 following the COVID crash recovery — both were strong bullish signals that preceded significant price appreciation. Throughout most of 2020–2021, the price traded well above both moving averages, confirming sustained bullish momentum. By late 2021, the price pulled back toward the 200-day SMA, suggesting a consolidation phase.
